# Public-domain and openly licensed media

Searches Wikimedia Commons and the Internet Archive (or one of them) and brings in up to six pictures, videos or
sounds, taking turns between the two. Only files you may use and change in any video come in: public domain (and
the Public Domain Mark), CC0, CC BY and CC BY-SA. Anything non-commercial (NC), "no derivatives" (ND), fair use,
unclear or unstated is left out, and the run's log says why.

Each file keeps its licence, its page, its author and the credit line its licence asks for — title, author,
licence and where it came from, e.g. “Harbour light” by Sea Painter, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons. A clip or
sound records them as its rights; a picture on its source. Delivering a video that uses them lists the credits in
the delivery's record and writes them into the `.notes.md` file next to the video, ready to paste into a
description. CC BY-SA asks that what you make from the file is shared under the same licence: check before using
one in a client's video.

- **Licence of the files:** per file, as above. Wikimedia sometimes adds restrictions (trademarks, a person's
  likeness); the run's log names them.
- **Licence of this ability's code:** MIT.
- **Cost:** free; no account and no key.
- **What leaves this computer:** the words you search for (and the contact you give, if any), to
  commons.wikimedia.org and archive.org. Files come from upload.wikimedia.org and the Internet Archive's servers
  (`*.archive.org`).
- **Who starts a run:** anyone, and Claude as a safe step: it is free, uses no key and sends only words.

Wikimedia asks every tool for a way to reach the person using it; give an email or web address as `contact` if you
run it often. Videos from Commons come as WebM (the original or a copy Wikimedia made, at most 1080 lines high);
videos from the Internet Archive as MP4. Files over 500 MB are left out. Old film and photographs search best with
period words: "harbour 1920s", "steam train", "city street 1950".
